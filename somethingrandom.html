About the Edmund Fitzgerald

The SS Edmund Fitzgerald was an American Great Lakes freighter launched in 1958.

At 729 feet in length, she was the largest ship on the Great Lakes when she entered service.

On November 10, 1975, the Fitzgerald sank in Lake Superior during a severe storm, taking all 29 crew members with her.

Her story continues to be remembered by maritime historians, Great Lakes enthusiasts, and people around the world.

History of the Mighty Fitz

The SS Edmund Fitzgerald was built by Great Lakes Engineering Works in River Rouge, Michigan.

She was launched on June 7, 1958, and named after Edmund Fitzgerald, then president of Northwestern Mutual Life Insurance Company.

SHIP INFORMATION
Name SS Edmund Fitzgerald
Launched June 7, 1958
Length 729 feet
Type Great Lakes Bulk Freighter
Primary Cargo Taconite / Iron Ore Pellets
Lost November 10, 1975
Crew 29

For seventeen years, the Fitzgerald transported iron ore across the Great Lakes, becoming a familiar sight at industrial ports throughout the region.

The Final Voyage - November 1975

NOVEMBER 9, 1975

The Fitzgerald departed Superior, Wisconsin, carrying approximately 26,000 tons of taconite pellets. Her destination was Detroit, Michigan.

NOVEMBER 10, 1975

A powerful storm swept across Lake Superior. The Fitzgerald encountered worsening conditions while traveling alongside the freighter Arthur M. Anderson.

Captain Ernest McSorley reported difficulties, including damage and the loss of radar capability.

At approximately 7:10 PM, the Fitzgerald made her last radio communication.

The vessel subsequently disappeared from radar. All 29 men aboard were lost.

The exact cause of the sinking remains debated. Several explanations have been proposed through official investigations and subsequent research.

Remembering the Crew

IN MEMORY OF THE 29

November 10, 1975

Twenty-nine men lost their lives aboard the SS Edmund Fitzgerald.

This website is dedicated to their memory and to the families who remember them.
